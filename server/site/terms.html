6. Account and recovery

There is no user account in the usual sense: your access is a 24-word recovery phrase generated on your device. We do not know it and cannot restore it. Whoever holds the phrase holds the credit. Keep it safe.

What we keep about a plan. A plan has to stay linked to the account it belongs to — otherwise it could not renew. For as long as it runs we hold, per account: the plan level, the reference of the subscription at Stripe or of Apple's transaction, the end of the period that has been paid for, and the month's allowance. For a code, the link between the code and the account that redeemed it is deleted after seven days. Payment records (amount, date, payment method, country) are kept for bookkeeping. None of this touches your prompts: they are paid with blind-signed coins that carry no identifier, so nothing on our side connects an account to what was asked.

Plans in the iPhone app. On iPhone, a plan is bought through the App Store. Apple is the seller of record for that payment and its price is set in the App Store; Apple's terms, cancellation and refund rules apply to it. The allowance itself is granted by us the moment Apple's transaction is verified. Restore purchases in the app brings a running plan back — also onto a new recovery phrase, if the old one is lost; the plan then moves to the new account.

6a. Validity of credit drawn onto a device

To spend TOKU, the app draws it from your account onto your device as coins: blind-signed bearer tokens that we cannot trace back to you, which is what keeps your purchases and your prompts apart. Coins carry a validity date set when they are drawn, and they are spendable for at least 90 days from that day — often a few days longer, never less. This period is a property of the cryptographic scheme, not a commercial choice: a coin that could be spent forever would also have to be remembered forever by every server that must refuse it a second time.

Two dates, not one. The monthly allowance lapses at the end of its calendar month (section 3). Credit from codes, and credit bought before plans existed, does not lapse on your account. Coins moved back with Return to account balance return to where they came from: what was drawn from a month's allowance goes back into that allowance and lapses with it; only credit that never was allowance stays for good.

The app keeps this from costing you anything as long as it is opened now and then: it draws only a small amount at a time, moves unspent coins back to your account (or onto your recovery phrase) before they expire when it is opened in the days before the deadline, and shows a notice in the account view and, where you allow it, a local notification when coins on a device are about to expire. Coins that expire nonetheless are void and are not refunded, because we cannot tell which coins were spent and which were not. The amount at risk is by design never more than the credit most recently drawn onto that device.
