6. Account and recovery

There is no user account in the usual sense: your access is a 24-word recovery phrase generated on your device. We do not know it and cannot restore it. Whoever holds the phrase holds the credit. Keep it safe.

What we keep about a purchase. For seven days after it settles, a purchase stays linked to the account it was made from — or, for a code bought on the website, to the code it produced. That is the window in which we can trace a payment that did not arrive and judge a refund. After seven days the link is deleted. The purchase record itself (amount, date, payment method, country) is kept for bookkeeping. None of this touches your prompts: the account a purchase belongs to and the session your prompts are billed to are not connected on our side.

Purchases in the iPhone app. On iPhone, credit is bought through the App Store. The credit itself is delivered by us the moment Apple's transaction is verified, and from then on it is credit like any other: on your recovery phrase, spent as coins, not refundable by us once used.

6a. Validity of credit drawn onto a device

Credit on your account does not expire. To spend it, the app draws it onto your device as coins: blind-signed bearer tokens that we cannot trace back to you, which is what keeps your purchases and your prompts apart. Coins carry a validity date set when they are issued, at most 90 days ahead and never less than roughly three months' worth of ordinary use. This period is a property of the cryptographic scheme, not a commercial choice: a coin that could be spent forever would also have to be remembered forever by every server that must refuse it a second time.

Only coins carry a date. Credit you have not drawn onto a device, and credit you move back to your account with Return to account balance, does not expire: it is held on your account and reached by your recovery phrase alone.

The app keeps this from costing you anything as long as it is opened now and then: it draws only a small amount at a time, moves unspent coins back to your account (or onto your recovery phrase) before they expire when it is opened in the days before the deadline, and shows a notice in the account view and, where you allow it, a local notification when coins on a device are about to expire. Coins that expire nonetheless are void and are not refunded, because we cannot tell which coins were spent and which were not. The amount at risk is by design never more than the credit most recently drawn onto that device.
